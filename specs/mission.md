# Mission

**Torre de Control** is a real-time operations web app for the **Admissions Service (Servicio de Admisión) of Hospital Clínico San Carlos, Madrid**. The Admissions Service is the hospital's control tower: it decides where every patient goes, when beds free up, and how patients move between the Emergency Department, wards and other units. Torre de Control gives that team — and hospital management — a live, shared picture of what is happening right now.

## Purpose

Run the hospital **the way Amazon runs fulfillment**:

| Amazon principle | What it means in the hospital |
|---|---|
| **Track & trace every package** | Every patient journey is a timeline of events (arrived, triaged, admit decision, bed assigned, in transfer, in bed, discharge ordered, discharged) visible to anyone who needs it. |
| **Promise dates & ETAs** | Every bed has an expected discharge date; every bed request has an ETA; every transfer has a pickup ETA. |
| **SLAs and exception management** | Each flow has a target time (triage level targets, boarding time, transfer pickup, bed turnaround). Breaches raise alerts automatically instead of being discovered by phone. |
| **Single source of truth, event-driven** | All state changes are recorded as immutable events; dashboards are projections of those events. |
| **Dispatch queues** | Internal transfers are dispatched to porters (celadores) like delivery routes, with live status. |
| **Input metrics over output metrics** | Dashboards emphasise controllable drivers (discharges before noon, bed turnaround, boarding time) not just occupancy. |
| **Forecasting** | Bed balance for the day (expected admissions − expected discharges) today; ML-based demand forecasting later. |

## Product Scope (MVP)

The MVP is a **Progressive Web App backed by IndexedDB** and an **in-browser event simulator**, built to be demoed to the hospital. It covers four operational areas plus an overview:

1. **Bed management (Gestión de camas)** — live census per unit, bed status map, occupancy, cleaning, blocked beds, expected discharges.
2. **Emergency Department (Urgencias)** — patients present by triage level and phase, waiting times vs targets, patients pending admission (boarding).
3. **Admissions & discharges (Ingresos y altas)** — scheduled vs urgent admissions, discharges of the day, discharges before noon, daily bed balance forecast.
4. **Internal transfers (Traslados internos)** — request queue, porter dispatch and live "order tracking" of each transfer.
5. **Control tower overview** — hospital-wide KPIs, status level, alert feed, wall-display (TV) mode.

All data in the MVP is **synthetic**, generated by a deterministic simulator. A **simulated login** with fictitious users and roles shows how each role sees the hospital.

## Primary Audience

- **Admissions Service staff** (jefe/a de admisión, gestores/as de camas) — the day-to-day operators.
- **Unit nurse supervisors (supervisión de enfermería)** — need their unit's beds, expected discharges and incoming patients.
- **ED coordination (coordinación de Urgencias)** — needs boarding and flow information.
- **Porters / transfer coordination (celadores)** — need their dispatch queue.
- **Hospital management (Dirección / Gerencia)** — read-only, hospital-wide view.

Secondary audience: the developer (and coding agents) building it. The codebase must be clean, layered and easy to extend, because the persistence layer will be replaced after the MVP.

## Stakeholder Voices (assumed — to be validated in interviews with the hospital)

- **Head of Admissions:** "I want to stop finding out about free beds by phone. I want to see the whole hospital at a glance and act on exceptions."
- **Bed managers:** "Tell me which patients are waiting for a bed, which beds will free up today, and which beds are dirty."
- **ED coordination:** "Patients waiting for a ward bed block my cubicles. Show how long they have been waiting and make it visible upstairs."
- **Nurse supervisors:** "Show me who is coming to my unit and when, and let me flag expected discharges early."
- **Porters:** "Give me a clear queue of pickups with origin, destination and priority."
- **Management:** "One screen with the status of the hospital, trends and where it hurts."
- **Hospital IT:** "Patient data must never leave our infrastructure; integration must use standard interfaces (HL7/FHIR); the app must fit our security framework (ENS) and GDPR."

## Non-Goals (MVP)

- No real patient data, ever, in the MVP (IndexedDB is unencrypted browser storage).
- No clinical records, prescriptions or diagnoses beyond a coarse specialty/reason label.
- No real authentication or authorisation (login is simulated and clearly labelled as such).
- No integration with the hospital information system (HIS) — the simulator stands in for it.
- No billing, scheduling of outpatient clinics or surgery planning.

## Guiding Principles

1. **Event first.** Every change is a domain event; current state is a projection. This makes the later HL7 ADT / FHIR integration a matter of mapping messages to events.
2. **Ports & adapters.** UI and domain never depend on IndexedDB/Dexie directly; storage sits behind repository interfaces so it can be swapped for PostgreSQL/Supabase.
3. **Exceptions over data dumps.** Every dashboard answers "what needs my attention now?" before "what are all the numbers?".
4. **Glanceable and calm.** Designed for wall displays and busy people: big numbers, clear colour semantics, no noise.
5. **Demo-able at any moment.** The simulator, seeded and deterministic, makes every phase showable.
6. **Privacy by design.** Synthetic data now; minimal data, audit and on-premise hosting later.

## What Success Looks Like

- **MVP:** a 10-minute demo in which the Head of Admissions watches a simulated busy morning — ED arrivals, admit decisions, discharges, cleaning, transfers — and recognises their hospital, sees exceptions surface automatically, and asks to pilot it.
- **Post-MVP:** the app runs on the hospital's infrastructure, fed by real ADT events, and measurably reduces ED boarding time and bed turnaround time.
